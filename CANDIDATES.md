# Candidates — Make the AI Judge Laugh

Brief applied to every candidate, in order: (1) clearly about office
life and lands as a joke, (2) observation true to the office so the
listener nods first, (3) surprising turn in the last words, (4) tight
enough to read aloud in one breath, (5) fresh rather than a stock line
with the nouns swapped. Crowded angles in the visible field (mute
buttons, printers, focus-time meetings, standup length, coffee,
meetings about meetings, fridge lunches, reply-all threads) were
avoided.

1. "I volunteered to take meeting notes and now I own every action item I wrote down." — WINNER (81 chars). Truth: the note-taker really does inherit the tasks they record, so the nod comes first. Turn: volunteering, the helpful act, is the trap, and the punch lands on the self-inflicted final words. Tight: one breath, no wasted setup. Fresh: notes/action-items is not a stock office-joke angle.
2. "We got ergonomic chairs and now I can be uncomfortable in 14 adjustable ways." — Runner-up. Fresh image and tight, but the truth nod is softer than the winner's and the laugh is a smirk rather than a story turn.
3. "I replied 'sounds good' to a plan I hadn't read and now I'm presenting it Thursday." — Very true and tight, but the unread-agreement trope is more familiar than the winner's mechanism.
4. "Our office plant died, so HR sent a survey asking if we felt supported." — Surprising escalation, but the plant-to-survey link is looser, so it loses on truth before surprise can save it.
5. "HR said bring your whole self to work, so I brought the self that naps at 2pm and they sent him home." — Good turn on a real corporate phrase, but longer and the phrase itself is well worn.
6. "I took a sick day to rest and spent it answering 'rest up!' emails from people copying me in." — True, but the turn is gentler and CC humour is crowded.
7. "I set my status to 'Do Not Disturb' and three people messaged to ask if it was working." — Tight, but status jokes are a familiar family.
8. "I asked IT for a second monitor and got a form asking why one screen wasn't enough for two jobs." — True form-culture observation, but the setup spends more words for a smaller payoff.
9. "Our open-plan office is so collaborative that I know everyone's lunch order and nobody's deadlines." — Nice contrast, but it reads as an observation more than a joke with a turn.
10. "I finally learned everyone's name, then we reorganized." — Tight and true, but the turn is mild; it would lose head-to-head against any candidate with a sharper final beat.

Pairwise result: candidate 1 beats 2 on truth and story turn, beats 3
on freshness, and beats 4 on truth. It is the submitted line.
